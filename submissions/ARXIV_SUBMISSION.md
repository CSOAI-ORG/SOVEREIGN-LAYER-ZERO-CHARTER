**Title:**
The Sovereign Layer Zero Charter: A Public, Auditable Interoperability Layer Unifying MCP, A2A, ACP, AGNTCY, x402, L2B, and L2L

**Authors:**
Nicholas Templeman (CSOAI Ltd, UK 16939677)

**Abstract (250 words):**
Agent interoperability today is fragmented across at least seven major protocol families (Anthropic MCP, Google A2A, IBM ACP, the Linux Foundation AGNTCY, Coinbase x402, plus ad-hoc LLM-to-Backend and LLM-to-LLM flows). Each fragment is technically defensible; collectively they create a sovereign-computing void that is currently being filled by private nation-state programs whose audit trails are not publicly inspectable. We present the **Sovereign Layer Zero Charter v1.0**, a single public document under Creative Commons CC0 1.0 that **subsumes** — without breaking — all seven protocol families into one Unified Wire Envelope (UWE) and binds them to one Ed25519 trust root, one SIGIL hash-chained event ledger, and one OrgKernel three-layer (L1 identity, L2 execution, L3 compliance assertion) audit chain. The Charter formally defines two previously undefined protocols (L2B and L2L) and aligns the unified layer with eight public frameworks (EU AI Act, GDPR, ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF, SOC 2, DORA, UK AI Bill, NIS2). Critically, every event emitted under the Charter is **verifiable in any web browser** by any citizen, via a public SIGIL inspection endpoint, without requiring installation. The Charter is bounded by immutable red lines excluding kinetic targeting and personal surveillance. Adoption is offered in six tiers, from zero-change (T0) to full SOVEREIGN-SEAL certification (T5); vendors stop at their chosen tier, regulators may require the top tier. The reference implementation is provided under Apache License 2.0. We argue that this Charter is the smallest credible public substrate capable of preventing a proprietary fragment-takeover of agent-layer interop in 2026-2028.
